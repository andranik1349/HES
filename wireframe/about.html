About HES

What this framework is, who built it, and what it deliberately excludes.

01 — The Framework

What HES is

The Human Elements System is a structured reference framework for the constituent elements of human psychology. It does not explain the mind — it catalogues, defines, and versions the psychological constructs from which explanations are built. Each element is assigned a code, a layer, a measurability level, and a set of nearest neighbours within the network.

HES is designed to serve as a stable common vocabulary for practitioners, researchers, and anyone who needs to think precisely about psychological capacities. It is not a personality system, a diagnostic instrument, or a clinical tool. It is a reference architecture.

The four-layer architecture

HES organises its 343 elements across four conceptually distinct layers, each representing a different kind of psychological phenomenon. The layers are not a hierarchy of importance — they reflect differences in stability, origin, and temporal character. Core elements are biologically grounded and slow to change; Emotional elements are fast-moving and context-sensitive; Quantum elements represent discontinuous rupture states rather than traits.

Each layer has its own internal block structure, coding convention, and measurability profile. The layers interact but are not reducible to one another.

History of the project

HES originated in a systematic dissatisfaction with the composite constructs that dominate psychological discourse — intelligence, resilience, character — each of which bundles multiple distinct capacities together in ways that resist clean measurement. The project began as a personal cataloguing effort, with the aim of building a list of psychological elements that could actually be individually defined and assessed.

Early iterations were loose and taxonomic. Over time the project developed the Measurability Level (MML) scale as a way of rating the evidential status of each element, and the four-layer architecture as a way of distinguishing phenomena that had previously been collapsed together. The current version represents the first stable public release of the full framework.

02 — The Author

Photo

Areg Oganesian

Areg Oganesian is an independent researcher and practitioner working at the intersection of psychological measurement, cognitive science, and applied frameworks for human development. His work focuses on the problem of construct validity — specifically, on how psychological concepts are defined, measured, and misused. Over two decades of clinical and research practice led him to conclude that the field lacked a shared reference architecture for individual psychological elements: a catalogue rigorous enough to distinguish constructs that are genuinely separable from those that are composites of more basic phenomena. The Human Elements System is his attempt to build that architecture from first principles, applying a consistent measurability standard across all 343 elements in the framework.

03 — Framework Boundaries

HES defines itself as much by what it excludes as by what it includes. The Stop List contains constructs deliberately kept out; the Parking Lot contains constructs deferred for future consideration.

Stop List — 10 deliberate exclusions

  • Intelligence (IQ)

    Excluded as a composite rather than a constituent element. HES maps the underlying capacities from which IQ scores are derived.

  • Personality types

    Type-based systems impose discrete categories on continuous traits. HES uses dimensional constructs only.

  • Diagnostic categories (DSM/ICD)

    Clinical diagnoses are administrative constructs, not psychological elements. HES does not map to diagnostic criteria.

  • Emotional intelligence (EI)

    EI is a composite of several HES elements already present in the framework. Including it as a separate construct would create redundancy.

  • Resilience

    Currently treated as an emergent property of several Derived and Quantum elements rather than a standalone construct.

  • Mindfulness

    A practice, not a construct. The underlying attentional and interoceptive elements are represented separately.

  • Grit

    Correlates heavily with existing persistence and motivation elements. Insufficient construct validity to warrant separate inclusion.

  • Character strengths (VIA)

    Values-based taxonomy with limited measurement infrastructure. May be revisited when MML criteria can be applied.

  • Flow states

    A phenomenon rather than a stable capacity. Considered for the Quantum layer but deferred pending construct clarification.

  • Neurodiversity categories

    Categorical labels rather than dimensional constructs. The underlying element-level differences are represented within the framework.

Parking Lot — 7 deferred constructs

  • Moral reasoning

    Construct boundaries unclear at this time. Strong candidate for the Derived layer pending further review.

  • Aesthetic sensitivity

    Partially represented in the Emotional layer. Full inclusion requires additional measurement work.

  • Narrative identity

    Overlaps with Identity & Self block. Under consideration as a standalone element or block-level expansion.

  • Sleep quality

    Biologically grounded but MML evidence inconsistent. Under review for the Core layer.

  • Interoception accuracy

    Distinguished from interoceptive sensitivity; measurement standards not yet sufficient.

  • Cognitive reserve

    Strong theoretical case for the Core layer. Deferred pending lifespan measurement standardisation.

  • Social rhythm

    Promising construct for the Derived layer. Currently lacks sufficient independent validation studies.
